# Progress

One entry per completed stage of docs/BRIEF.md.

- 2026-11-16: repository set up with CLAUDE.md, build brief and reference material (Chapter 1 prototype, HUD and feel-layer experiments, workout files).
- 2026-11-16: **Stage 0 done.** Vite + TypeScript + Vitest + ESLint skeleton. `npm run dev`, `npm test`, `npm run build`, `npm run lint` all work. ESLint blocks DOM globals and Three.js imports under `src/core/`, and the build type-checks the core a second time without the DOM library, so portability is enforced rather than promised.
- 2026-11-16: **Stage 1 done.** Workout engine in `src/core/workout`: model with steady, ramp, interval sets and free ride; `.zwo` parser and writer (own DOM-free XML reader); FIT workout parser and writer on a shared FIT codec in `src/core/fit` (decoder, writer, CRC); a time-stateless `WorkoutRunner` with targets, next step, text events and "spin up in 5" cues. Both reference workouts parse (Storm Front: 52 steps, 60 min; CrushCh1: 37 steps, 35 min, 7 efforts at 100%+). Note: `CrushCh1.fit` and the prototype's interval table in `a_core.js` differ in places (step boundaries and some targets); Stage 4 treats `a_core.js` as the Chapter 1 source, as the brief says.
- 2026-11-16: **Stage 2 done.** `src/core/record`: a 1 Hz `RideRecorder` driven by the caller's clock (power, cadence, heart rate, game speed, realistic bike speed and distance from a flat-road physics model, lap markers); `summarise` (duration, average and normalised power, kJ, IF, TSS, heart rate, time in power and HR zones); and a FIT activity encoder (file_id, file_creator, device_info, timer events, records, laps, session, activity, CRC). Tests decode the export with the independent `fit-file-parser` library and check NP and kJ against hand-computed fixtures. Not yet verified: an actual upload to Garmin Connect or Strava (needs a real account upload; listed in the hardware checklist).
- 2026-11-16: **Stage 3 done.** `src/devices`: `PowerSource`, `HeartRateSource` and `ControllableTrainer` interfaces; pure packet code for FTMS (Indoor Bike Data, Control Point, status, power range, features), Cycling Power (with crank-revolution cadence maths) and Heart Rate, all tested against byte fixtures written from the specs; `FtmsTrainer` control logic (write queue, response matching, re-requesting control) shared by Bluetooth and the simulator; Web Bluetooth plumbing with reconnect that restores the last target, and a Cycling Power fallback; `SimTrainer` (speaks real FTMS bytes, ERG lag and noise, a rider who follows targets, manual override, stop pedalling) and `SimHeartRate` (lags power by about 30 s, slower recovery, drift); `DeviceManager`; and a device test page at `devices.html`. The Bluetooth code is tested against a fake GATT stack only: it has never met a real trainer. See `docs/TESTING-WITH-HARDWARE.md` (Stage 6).
- 2026-11-16: **Stage 4 done.** Chapter format and runtime. `schemas/chapter.schema.json` plus TypeScript types in `src/core/chapter`: metadata, workout (inline or file), and tracks for dialogue, glass, stills, HUD (titles, banners, notices, waypoints, hide, widgets, effects), scene (segments, camera, speed, environment, named curves, impacts, props, markers, streaks), sfx, music and author notes. `ChapterRuntime` gives the state of every track as a pure function of time and, per frame, the one-shot events crossed; a backwards step or a jump is treated as a seek (no one-shots refire, dialogue reports its mid-clip offset). Conditions on rider data (`challenges`) with hold time, grace, success and fail branches and story flags, exercised by `chapters/test-clamp.chapter.json`. `chapters/ch1.chapter.json` is generated from the prototype by `tools/port-ch1-prototype.ts` and tested against the prototype itself, which the tests load in a sandbox: intervals, speed, distance, camera, field of view, shake, environment, subtitles, glass, stills, beats, sounds and music direction all match. `tools/ride-script-to-chapter.ts` converts the writers' Markdown table to a draft and lists what it guessed; it handles the real Chapter 1 ride script (`test/fixtures/ch1-ride-script.md`, exported from the Claude Doc) and round-trips a sample table. `npm run validate` checks every chapter file. Note: the ride script and the prototype have drifted apart in places (the script still says Dax where the prototype says Holt, and some lines differ); `ch1.chapter.json` follows the prototype, as the brief asks.
- 2026-11-16: **Stage 5 done.** Chapter 1 plays on the engine. `src/web/ride`: `world.ts` (the prototype's Three.js scene kits, now reading scene, camera, speed, environment, curves, impacts, props and markers from the chapter), `audio.ts` (clips, the sound bank, the step sequencer and ambience layers, all driven by the chapter's `sfx`, `music` and `ambience` data; engine and wind follow the rider), `stage.ts` (the visor HUD from `reference/hud/visor-hud.html` plus stills, canopy, condensation and Glass writing, subtitles, title cards and story widgets) and `app.ts` (start screen with FTP, max HR, devices, feel preset and HUD position; the frame loop; controls; end screen with summary and **Download .fit**). The feel maths is in `src/core/feel` with presets off, restrained, standard and aggressive. Chapters are served as static data and fetched at run time (`?chapter=<id>`). Story time follows the wall clock; the rider's effort scales the speed read-out, streaks, engine and wind, so a rider who stops sees the ship sag while the story carries on (the trade-off is written up in `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`). Verified with `npm run check:ride`, which rides all 35 minutes in headless Chromium with the simulated rider (no console errors), checks seeking, pause and stop-pedalling, downloads the `.fit` and decodes it with an independent decoder; the test chapter's challenge also resolves and sets its flags. Not verified: how it looks and sounds on a real GPU at full frame rate (the headless run uses software rendering), and anything with a real trainer.
- 2026-11-16: **Stage 6 done.** Handover docs: `README.md` (clone to riding in five minutes, keys, commands), `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` (modules, data flow, the story-pace against rider-effort trade-off, what is portable to Unreal, known limits), `docs/AUTHORING.md` (how to write a chapter, track by track) and `docs/TESTING-WITH-HARDWARE.md` (checklist for the first session with a real trainer, strap and a `.fit` upload). Checked from a fresh clone: `npm install` took under a minute, tests and chapter validation pass, and `npm run dev` then **Start ride** has the simulated rider riding Chapter 1.

## Open items

Things the build could not settle, for whoever picks this up:

- **Real hardware.** Bluetooth (FTMS, Cycling Power, Heart Rate) is untested on a real trainer. Work through `docs/TESTING-WITH-HARDWARE.md`.
- **Platform uploads.** The `.fit` export decodes cleanly with an independent decoder but has not been uploaded to Garmin Connect or Strava.
- **Look and sound on a real machine.** Headless checks use software rendering and do not judge how it feels. The feel presets in particular want tuning by riding.
- **Chapter 1 source of truth.** `ch1.chapter.json` follows the prototype. The ride script has since moved on (Holt for Dax, some changed lines) and `CrushCh1.fit` differs from the prototype's intervals in places. Decide which wins, then edit the chapter file by hand and retire `tools/port-ch1-prototype.ts` and the tests that pin the chapter to the prototype.
- **Rider-driven distance.** The scenery keeps story pace when the rider stops; only the speed read-out, streaks and sound respond. See "Story pace against rider effort" in `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` for the route to chapters that wait for the rider.
