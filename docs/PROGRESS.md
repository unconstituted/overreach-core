## Stages 7 and 8

- 2026-11-17: **Stage 7a done.** Content types beyond chapters, in `content/`: workouts (`*.workout.json` with inline steps, or metadata beside a `.zwo` or `.fit`), plans (weeks of days of sessions referring to `workout:<id>` or `chapter:<id>`, with optional sessions and alternatives; calendar or story style), maps (scene kit, length, terrain profile) and seasons (ordered chapters, flags). Unlock rules are data from a fixed vocabulary (`always`, `chapterCompleted`, `flag`, `totalDistance`, `totalTime`, `planWeek`, `all`, `any`). Each type has a JSON schema and types in `src/core/content`. `buildCatalogue` indexes everything with the fields menus filter on (duration, estimated intensity and stress, hard-day flag, a power graph, plan length and so on) and is where cross-references are checked: a plan pointing at a missing workout, a season listing a missing chapter, a duplicate id or an unlock rule pointing nowhere fails `npm run validate`, `npm run catalogue` and `npm run build`. A Vite plugin rebuilds the catalogue in development, serves `content/` as static files and copies it beside the build. Placeholder content: 13 workouts across the main focuses (one loaded from a `.zwo`), three plans (Base Builder, the story-led Season 1 Campaign, Shakedown Week), three maps on existing scene kits, and Season 1.

- 2026-11-17: **Stage 8a done** (running, in the core). Workout targets can be power (fraction of FTP or watts), **pace** (fraction of threshold speed, or seconds per kilometre) or **heart rate** (fraction of maximum, or bpm); the runner reports each in its own terms plus a fraction of threshold, and cues "Pick it up in 5" for a runner. `.zwo` run files are read with their `Power` values as fractions of threshold pace (as Zwift means them); FIT workouts read and write speed and heart-rate targets. `src/core/workout/effort.ts` maps running pace (and heart rate) to an FTP-equivalent effort (80% of threshold pace ≈ 50% FTP, threshold = threshold, 108% ≈ 120%, incline adds about 3.5% per percent), so the feel layer, world speed, simulated heart, workout graphs and training stress are shared between the sports. `asRun` turns any cycling workout into the same session as a run at the same effort; Chapter 1 maps cleanly and keeps its cues. The feel layer takes a strike rate (one strike per running step). Run recording takes distance from the treadmill or footpod, scores a run without power from normalised speed, and the `.fit` export writes a run as sport 1, sub-sport treadmill, cadence as strides per minute with the half step in the fractional field, and leaves power out when there is none; it decodes as a treadmill run with the independent decoder.
- 2026-11-17: **Stage 8b done** (running devices). Byte-level parsers and encoders for **FTMS Treadmill Data** (0x2ACD), treadmill control (target speed and incline, start, stop), speed and incline status, supported speed and incline ranges, and the treadmill feature bits; and for **Running Speed and Cadence** (0x1814) footpods, reading pods that send strides per minute as steps. `FtmsTreadmill` holds the control logic: it clamps every target to the treadmill's range and to engine safety limits (20 km/h and 10% by default), and once the treadmill reports that the runner stopped the belt (stop button or safety key) it sends nothing more until the runner chooses to resume; it never restarts a stopped belt after a reconnect either. A treadmill with no control point connects read-only. `SimTreadmill` is a treadmill, a runner and a footpod speaking real bytes, with treadmill-rate acceleration, a runner who follows targets by hand when control is off, and a stop button. Web Bluetooth: `connectTreadmill` and `connectFootpod` (with running power over the Cycling Power Service when the pod offers it, as Stryd does). `DeviceManager` has a run mode: speed and incline from the treadmill, cadence and power from the footpod (speed from the pod when there is no treadmill), a treadmill-control switch, a `runnerStopped` event, and a simulated heart that follows running effort. All exercised against the simulator and a fake Web Bluetooth stack, including reconnect.

The visor shows pace as the hero number (per km or mile), SPM, target pace or heart rate, and "Pick it up"; the treadmill follows the workout, pauses with the game, and if the runner stops the belt themselves the run pauses and the belt is left alone until they press play. Settings: usual sport, treadmill control, treadmill top speed (18 km/h by default). The device panel and the device test page have run modes (treadmill, footpod, heart rate). Post-run screens, history and the `.fit` show distance, pace and cadence; runs without power are scored and zoned by effort.
